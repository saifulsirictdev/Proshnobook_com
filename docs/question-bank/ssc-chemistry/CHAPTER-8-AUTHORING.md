## Scientific conventions and safety

- Atomic masses for simple heat/mass checks:H1,C12,O16,Ca40. Conventional school STP0°C/1atm≈22.4L/mol is explicit in the retained sourceCQ answer. 1cal≈4.18J; this is an approximate classroom conversion.
- Gas-phase average bond energies are supplied and approximate. `ΔH≈ΣD(broken)−ΣD(formed)`; formation releases energy. Liquid versus gas H₂O is not silently interchanged. Enthalpy-level differences use a common arbitrary reference, not a claim of absolute internal energies.
- Electrolysis anode is positive/cathode negative; ideal galvanic anode negative/cathode positive. Oxidation remains at anode and reduction at cathode in **both**. Salt bridges conduct ions, external wires electrons.
- Aqueous active-metal salts do not universally deposit their metals. Soluble Cu/Ni/Ag anodes can replenish ions rather than emit gas. Plating items specify suitable controlled conditions; side reactions and impurities are not claimed absent in real industrial operation.
- Dry cell graphite is primarily a current collector; MnO₂ is active oxidant/depolarizer; the paste is moist, not completely water-free. Fuel cells convert chemical energy, solar cells light. Clean local products do not prove a zero-impact whole life cycle.
- Acid, reactive molten salts, toxic gas/barium/metal examples are theoretical teaching items. No home experiment, concentrated-acid preparation, battery disassembly or nuclear engineering instruction is provided. Nuclear examples are non-procedural curriculum-level conservation/energy explanations.
