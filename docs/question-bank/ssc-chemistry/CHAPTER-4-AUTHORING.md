## Scientific corrections and cautions

- Modern ordering is by **atomic number**, while neutral electron configurations explain periodic recurrence. Mendeleev’s historical mass/similarity classification and predictions retain their historical significance.
- H is in group1 but is not an alkali metal. **He is group18**, despite 1s2/s-block configuration. The source’s group2 comparison table that includes He is not treated as correct group placement.
- Neutral Cr `[Ar]3d5 4s1` → group6; Cu `[Ar]3d10 4s1` → group11. d-block group uses d+ns, not just outer-shell count. Ion shell occupations do not directly determine the parent element’s period.
- All d-block elements are not automatically transition elements. Zn and its common Zn2+ have d10; Cu can form d9 Cu2+. Variable oxidation/color/catalysis are common features, not guarantees for every compound.
- Detached lanthanoid/actinoid rows belong to periods6/7, not new periods8/9. No universal assignment of every f-series element as an ordinary group3 member is made; group3 boundaries/conventions should not be overgeneralized from the school layout.
- First IE generally increases across a main-group period but **Mg→Al and N→O are exceptions**, explained by subshell occupancy/pairing. Group trends have conditions and are not exact equal increments.
- EA is electron addition to a gas-phase atom; state the convention for released energy vs signed electron-attachment energy. **Cl releases more energy than F on ordinary electron attachment**, despite F having greater Pauling EN. Full s / half-full p and electron repulsion invalidate the source’s simple size-only EA ranking, including its Be/Mg example.
- EN refers to bonded shared electrons, not the same process/unit as isolated-atom EA or first IE. F is the Pauling maximum; noble-gas values are not blindly included in a universal trend.
- Noble gases are generally low-reactivity, not absolutely incapable of compounds; He’s closed shell has2, not8. Similar-group chemistry does not imply equal physical properties or identical water-reaction rates; Be/Mg/Ca behavior differs.
- Carbonate +acid equations are balanced; CO2 first precipitates CaCO3 in limewater, while excess CO2 can form soluble Ca(HCO3)2. A liquid seal is not permission to seal all gas outlets. No instructions to smell/handle gases, perform alkali-metal water reactions, or independently conduct acid/halogen experiments.
- Source edition dates and its118-element historical statement are not verification of an official examination year or current project facts.
