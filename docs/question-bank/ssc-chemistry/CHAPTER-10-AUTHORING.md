## Scientific and safety boundaries

- Bauxite and limonite formulas are supplied school models, not fixed pure compounds for every natural sample. Hydration water counts in supplied formula masses. A limestone Ca resource is not metallic Ca produced by ordinary carbon reduction of CaO.
- Fe₃O₄ contains Fe(II)/Fe(III); +8/3 is an average oxidation number, not every atom's fractional valency. Pyrite FeS₂ uses disulfide S₂²⁻ (mean S−1), unlike ZnS. CuFeS₂ uses the stated conventional Cu(I)/Fe(III)/S(−II) model.
- Extremely active metals use suitable molten-media electrolysis in the textbook route; aqueous brine does not simply plate metallic Na. Ordinary Mg carbon reduction is not the general school route; special industrial alternatives are not declared impossible.
- Cryolite is a solvent/bath aid, not merely a reaction-speed catalyst; carbon Al-cell anodes can be consumed. Refining is distinguished from extraction and ore concentration.
- Rust needs relevant water/oxygen conditions; salt accelerates corrosion as electrolyte. Zn versus Sn scratch behavior assumes electrical contact in a suitable wet electrolyte. Stainless/Al passivity is not all-environment immunity.
- Alloy percentages are given representative mass models, not universal grades. Karat does not alone specify hardness, companion metal identity or molar fraction.
- SO₂/SO₃/H₂SO₄ industrial steps are theoretical. Contact catalysis is not a guarantee of complete conversion. Mist/heat, concentrated-acid oxidizing/dehydrating properties and dilution are distinguished. H₂SO₃ and H₂S₂O₇ are qualified school summaries of more complex systems.
- No household toxic-gas/CO/Hg/Pb/acid production, acid dilution recipe, exposure test or chemical dosing instructions. pH alone never certifies metal-bearing wastewater safety. Recycling still needs sorting, energy and pollution control.
