## Scientific and safety caveats retained

- Iodine can sublime **and can be liquid under appropriate conditions** (including near 114°C at ordinary pressure). “Sublimable” does not mean never liquid. The source exercise's overgeneralized wording is not repeated as a universal truth.
- Heating NH4Cl involves dissociation to NH3/HCl and recombination on cooling; the school's apparent sublimation example is not represented as universally unchanged molecules simply evaporating.
- Boiling point depends on pressure. Nonvolatile-solute boiling-point elevation is not generalized to all volatile mixtures. Melting range/purity clues are not complete identification or toxicity tests.
- Diffusion, mechanical mixing and thermal convection are distinguished. Real macroscopic gas leaks may involve bulk flow rather than ideal molecular effusion.
- Native hazard-related illustrations are for **teacher-supervised demonstration or supplied-data analysis**, not instructions for students to handle cylinders, iodine, NH3, HCl, AlCl3, flames or hot glass. No smelling, tasting or flame-testing of gas is recommended.
- Automated checks do not certify scientific/teacher approval. Independent teacher review and official syllabus/mark-distribution verification remain outstanding.
