## ssc-p2-composition-001 — The Importance of Reading Newspapers

Original: Dhaka · Board 2024 · physical PDF page 2 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "The Importance of Reading Newspapers".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 229 words

### Possible model answer

A newspaper brings reports, analysis and opinions about events beyond our immediate surroundings. Although many people now read news on a screen, the basic purpose remains similar: helping readers understand what is happening and why it matters. For a student, regular and thoughtful reading can connect classroom learning with the wider world.

Newspapers cover subjects such as education, agriculture, science, business and sport. A report about a river may make a geography lesson more meaningful, while an interview can introduce an unfamiliar occupation. Reading also develops vocabulary and shows how writers organise information. I keep a small notebook for useful expressions instead of trying to memorise whole articles.

However, published material should not be accepted without judgement. News reporting, advertising and opinion serve different purposes. We should check dates, look for evidence and compare important claims with other reliable sources. A dramatic headline may leave out essential context. Respect for privacy and fair treatment of people mentioned in reports are also important standards.

My routine is to spend a short period reading after school, choosing a few substantial items rather than endless updates. This leaves time for lessons, recreation and rest. Newspapers cannot replace textbooks or direct experience, but they can encourage informed discussion. Their greatest value is not simply providing facts for examinations; it is helping us become curious, responsible readers who ask sensible questions about public life.

## ssc-p2-composition-002 — Your Favourite Hobby

Original: Rajshahi · Board 2024 · physical PDF page 4 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "Your Favourite Hobby".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 238 words

### Possible model answer

My favourite hobby is gardening. I began with two small pots on our veranda and gradually learned how much attention a living plant requires. Gardening gives me a peaceful activity outside lessons and screens. It also makes ordinary changes in the weather feel more interesting because I can see their effects directly.

I grow a few flowers and herbs in containers that allow water to drain. Before planting, I ask an experienced relative about suitable soil and available sunlight. I keep a simple record of watering and new growth. When a plant looks unhealthy, I try to understand the cause instead of immediately adding chemicals or too much water.

This hobby teaches patience. Seeds do not grow faster because I become impatient, and some plants fail despite my efforts. Those failures encourage observation and better planning. Gardening also develops responsibility: someone must care for the plants during examinations or a family visit. Sharing a healthy seedling with a neighbour is especially satisfying because it extends the pleasure beyond our home.

A hobby should fit a person's circumstances. A large garden and expensive equipment are not necessary; a safe corner and a few suitable containers can be enough. I do not let gardening replace study or needed rest. Instead, it gives balance to my routine. I hope to learn more about native plants and continue a hobby that combines practical work, quiet enjoyment and respect for the natural world.

## ssc-p2-composition-003 — The Season You Like Most

Original: Jashore · Board 2024 · physical PDF page 6 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "The Season You Like Most".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 232 words

### Possible model answer

Of the seasons I experience in Bangladesh, winter is my favourite. I enjoy its cooler air and the gentle sunlight that often follows a misty morning. After the heat and heavy rain of other months, the season can make outdoor activities more comfortable. My preference is personal, however, and winter is not equally pleasant for everyone.

On holidays, I like walking with my family after the mist begins to clear. Dew shines on the grass, and familiar fields look different in the softer light. Winter vegetables add variety to meals, while homemade cakes make family gatherings especially enjoyable. The season also provides opportunities for school sports, visits and cultural events when conditions are suitable.

Nevertheless, cold weather can cause serious difficulty for people without warm clothing or adequate shelter. Older people, young children and outdoor workers may need particular support. We should not turn another person's hardship into a picturesque detail. Sharing usable clothes through a reliable local organisation and checking on vulnerable neighbours are practical ways to show consideration.

I try to enjoy winter responsibly by wearing suitable clothes and keeping a balanced routine. A beautiful morning is best appreciated without ignoring the people working around us. My affection for the season comes from its quiet atmosphere, family traditions and opportunities to be outdoors. It also reminds me that enjoyment becomes more meaningful when it is accompanied by awareness and kindness.

## ssc-p2-composition-004 — A Journey You Have Recently Made

Original: Cumilla · Board 2024 · physical PDF page 8 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "A Journey You Have Recently Made".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 237 words

### Possible model answer

During a recent school holiday, I travelled by train with my mother to visit relatives. We bought our tickets in advance and reached the station early. Carrying a small bag made the journey easier. I had travelled by road before, so I looked forward to seeing familiar countryside from a different route.

The platform was busy with families and railway staff. We checked the coach number and boarded only after the train stopped. Once we found our seats, I placed the bag where it would not block the passage. Soon the train moved away, and buildings gradually gave way to fields, ponds and clusters of trees.

I watched people working beside the tracks and children returning from school. At some stations, passengers left and new travellers joined us. My mother explained why announcements and safety instructions matter, especially at crossings and crowded platforms. We kept our rubbish in a small bag instead of throwing it outside. A short delay reminded us to remain patient and keep our relatives informed.

We arrived tired but cheerful and found my uncle waiting beyond the platform. The journey was enjoyable not because everything happened perfectly, but because it gave us time to observe and talk. It also showed the importance of clean coaches, accessible stations and accurate travel information. I returned with pleasant memories and a better understanding that a successful journey depends on both responsible passengers and well-managed transport services.

## ssc-p2-composition-005 — Your Favourite Game

Original: Chattogram · Board 2024 · physical PDF page 10 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "Your Favourite Game".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 237 words

### Possible model answer

Football is my favourite game because it combines movement, planning and cooperation. A simple match can begin with a ball, a safe open space and agreed rules. I usually play with friends in the late afternoon. The enjoyment comes not only from scoring but also from passing well and helping the whole team improve.

In a standard match, two teams try to score by putting the ball into the opposing goal. Most players use their feet, while the goalkeeper has particular responsibilities within the rules. Positions and tactics help organise play, but a good player must notice changing situations. Communication and awareness are often more useful than trying to do everything alone.

Playing regularly can support fitness, coordination and social confidence when the activity suits the participants. We warm up first, use appropriate footwear and stop if someone is hurt. A friendly match should not become an excuse for dangerous tackles, insults or arguments. Respecting the referee and accepting defeat are important lessons, even when a decision disappoints us.

Football also has limitations. Excessive practice can interfere with study or recovery, and competitions can exclude beginners if winning becomes the only goal. Our group tries to give everyone a fair opportunity. I hope to improve my control and passing while keeping the game enjoyable. For me, football is most valuable when it builds friendship, discipline and generosity rather than merely adding another result to a score sheet.

## ssc-p2-composition-006 — Satellite Television Channels and Young People

Original: Rajshahi · Board 2020 · physical PDF page 49 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "Satellite Television Channels and Young People".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 239 words

### Possible model answer

Satellite television channels have widened the range of programmes available to many households. Young viewers can encounter news, documentaries, sport, drama and languages from different parts of the world. This variety creates opportunities for learning, but it also makes thoughtful selection important. Watching many channels is not the same as understanding what they present.

Educational programmes can make unfamiliar subjects clearer through pictures and explanation. A nature documentary may encourage interest in wildlife, while an interview can introduce a different viewpoint. Watching selected material with family members can lead to useful discussion. Subtitles and careful listening may also support language learning when the content is suitable for the viewer.

On the other hand, advertisements may encourage unnecessary purchases, and some programmes contain misleading information or material unsuitable for younger audiences. Continuous viewing can replace sleep, study and physical activity. We should distinguish entertainment from evidence and recognise that fictional situations do not always reflect ordinary life. Young people need guidance without being treated as incapable of making sensible choices.

In our home, we try to choose programmes beforehand rather than leave the television running throughout the evening. We discuss doubtful claims and switch off when a programme no longer interests us. Broadcasters also have responsibilities concerning accuracy and age-appropriate content. Satellite television is neither a complete education nor an unavoidable danger. Its influence depends on the programmes offered, the time spent watching and the critical habits that viewers develop.

## ssc-p2-composition-007 — The Importance of Physical Exercise

Original: Cumilla · Board 2020 · physical PDF page 53 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "The Importance of Physical Exercise".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 232 words

### Possible model answer

Physical exercise is planned movement that helps maintain or improve fitness. Walking, cycling, swimming and suitable games are familiar examples. For students, movement can provide a refreshing change from long periods of sitting. Exercise is not a competition to prove personal worth, and the best activity depends on a person's age, health, ability and interests.

Regular activity can support strength, coordination and general well-being. Team games may develop cooperation, while an individual walk can offer quiet time to think. I enjoy a short walk and friendly games because they are easy to fit around lessons. Being active also encourages me to notice the value of safe paths, clean playing areas and accessible community spaces.

Sensible preparation matters. We should use suitable equipment, drink water when needed and avoid pushing through serious pain or illness. A person with a medical concern may need professional advice about appropriate activity. Rest and adequate nutrition are important too. Exercise cannot guarantee freedom from disease, and expensive products promising instant fitness should be treated cautiously.

Schools can make movement enjoyable by offering varied activities instead of rewarding only the strongest athletes. Families can support routines without shaming anyone about body size. I want exercise to remain a sustainable part of my life, not a short-lived attempt at perfection. Its value lies in helping us participate more comfortably in everyday activities while balancing effort, enjoyment and necessary rest.

## ssc-p2-composition-008 — The Duties of a Student

Original: Sylhet · Board 2020 · physical PDF page 57 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "The Duties of a Student".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 237 words

### Possible model answer

A student's main responsibility is to learn, but learning is broader than collecting examination marks. It involves understanding ideas, asking questions and developing judgement. Students also belong to families, classrooms and communities. Their duties should therefore include honesty and consideration for others without placing unreasonable adult responsibilities on young people.

In academic work, I try to follow a manageable routine, attend lessons and seek help when I do not understand. Completing an assignment honestly is more useful than copying a polished answer. Revision works best when it checks understanding rather than repeats words mechanically. Caring for books and respecting deadlines also makes shared learning more organised.

Beyond lessons, students can help maintain a welcoming school. They should avoid bullying, respect differences and report harmful behaviour through safe channels. Suitable games, creative activities and voluntary work can develop cooperation. Small acts, such as helping a classmate find a resource, may matter more than making grand promises. Rest, friendship and emotional well-being remain essential parts of student life.

Families and teachers should guide students instead of demanding constant perfection. Not every learner has the same resources or circumstances. I believe a responsible student makes a sincere effort, learns from mistakes and treats other people fairly. These habits prepare us not only for an examination but also for future decisions. Education succeeds when it helps us become capable, thoughtful people who can contribute without losing compassion for ourselves or others.

## ssc-p2-composition-009 — Students and Social Service

Original: Mymensingh · Board 2020 · physical PDF page 63 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "Students and Social Service".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 239 words

### Possible model answer

Students can contribute to their communities through useful, age-appropriate social service. Such work should respond to a genuine need rather than exist only for photographs or certificates. It can connect education with everyday life and help learners understand experiences different from their own. Participation should be safe, voluntary and compatible with study and rest.

A school group might organise a reading session for younger children, help maintain a shared garden or collect suitable books for a community library. Students can also share verified information about local services under responsible supervision. Before starting, we should ask what people actually need. Offering unwanted goods or making promises that cannot be kept can create extra work for others.

Good intentions are not enough. Activities require permission, clear responsibilities and respect for privacy. Students should not enter floodwater, provide medical treatment or handle dangerous waste without appropriate expertise and protection. Adults and established organisations must lead work involving significant risks. Records of donations should be transparent, and recipients should never be pressured to pose for publicity.

Social service teaches cooperation, practical planning and humility. It shows that people facing difficulty are not simply objects of charity; they have knowledge, preferences and rights. I would like to help with a regular reading programme because consistent support may be more useful than a single event. The strongest form of student service combines willingness to help with careful listening, realistic commitments and respect for human dignity.

## ssc-p2-composition-010 — Your Aim in Life

Original: Dhaka · Board 2019 · physical PDF page 65 · Q15 · 12 original marks · SSC English 2nd Paper_2018_2024.pdf

Source-backed adapted practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "Your Aim in Life".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 242 words

### Possible model answer

My aim in life is to become a teacher. I admire the way a thoughtful teacher can make a difficult idea understandable and help a learner gain confidence. This ambition is not a prediction that my future is already fixed. It is a direction that gives purpose to my present efforts and may develop as I learn more.

I especially enjoy explaining lessons to younger relatives. Their questions often reveal gaps in my own understanding, so helping them also teaches me patience. A good teacher needs strong subject knowledge, clear communication and the ability to listen. Fairness matters because students come from different backgrounds and do not all learn at the same pace.

To prepare, I must study carefully and improve both my language skills and my understanding of the subjects I may teach. I also want to read widely and observe how effective teachers organise a class. Professional training will be necessary; kindness alone cannot replace the knowledge required for responsible teaching. Equally, excellent marks cannot excuse humiliation or unfair treatment of learners.

I hope to work where students have limited access to good learning support. However, I know that one person cannot solve every educational problem. Schools need resources, supportive leadership and cooperation with families. Whatever occupation I eventually enter, I want to carry the values behind this aim: patience, curiosity and service. A worthwhile ambition should guide our growth without making us forget the dignity of other people's work.

Web-topic-backed authored practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "Adult Literacy".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 240 words

### Possible model answer

Adult literacy involves helping adults develop useful reading, writing and numeracy skills. Some people missed educational opportunities because of poverty, displacement, disability or responsibilities during childhood. Their lack of formal schooling does not mean they lack intelligence or practical knowledge. A respectful programme begins by recognising the experience adults already bring to learning.

Literacy can support everyday tasks such as reading instructions, understanding a notice, checking a transaction or helping a child with schoolwork. It may also increase confidence in using public services. The goals should reflect learners' actual priorities rather than only the convenience of a textbook. An adult who manages a household or farm may need different examples from those used in a primary classroom.

Programmes require suitable times, accessible locations and patient, trained facilitators. Learners should not be embarrassed by childish treatment or public comparison. Printed materials need clear language and relevant content. Progress may be gradual because adults must balance lessons with work and care responsibilities. Continued opportunities to use skills are essential; a short course without follow-up may not provide lasting confidence.

Students can support established activities through supervised reading practice or the preparation of useful materials. They should avoid promising results beyond their abilities. Literacy is not a substitute for fair wages or reliable services, but it can widen choice. A society values education fully when learning remains possible throughout life and people are welcomed back without shame about the opportunities they missed earlier.

## ssc-p2-composition-051 — A Visit to a Book Fair

Web-topic-backed authored practice. Write a short composition of about 250 words on "A Visit to a Book Fair".

Current practice: 20 marks · target about 250 words · model 243 words

### Possible model answer

A book fair brings readers, publishers and writers together in a lively public space. Last year, I visited a local fair with my sister and a limited amount of spending money. We planned to explore before buying anything. This simple decision helped us look beyond colourful covers and choose books that genuinely interested us.

The stalls offered fiction, poetry, children's literature and books on science and history. At one stall, a bookseller asked about my reading experience and suggested two different collections. I read a few pages of each before deciding. We also attended a short discussion about translating stories, which showed how much care is involved in carrying ideas from one language to another.

The fair was enjoyable, but good organisation mattered. Clear signs, accessible paths, safe drinking water and places to rest helped visitors stay comfortable. Books should also be available at different prices so that reading does not become a display of purchasing power. A fair can encourage library membership and borrowing as well as sales. Not everyone needs to leave carrying a large bag.

I returned home with one story collection and a list of titles to seek later. The visit made reading feel like a shared activity rather than a solitary duty connected only with examinations. A successful book fair creates curiosity that continues after the stalls close. Its greatest achievement is helping people find a book, an idea or a conversation that encourages them to keep reading.
