### mcq

1. A. To learn more about him

The introduction states the teacher wanted to know more about him.

2. B. Collecting stamps and reading

His form distinguishes hobbies from the activities he is good at.

3. C. Soft drinks

The last statement in his form identifies soft drinks.

4. D. By rickshaw with his father

The narrative names both the vehicle and his companion.

5. A. Fifteen minutes

The passage states that they reached school after fifteen minutes.

6. B. He found a seat

Finding a seat follows entering the classroom in the narrative.

7. C. The pupils were friendly and the teacher greeted the class warmly

The welcome is supported by classroom behaviour, not travel details.

### poem

1. The textbook credits Kate Greenaway.

Names the credited poet, without attributing authorship to the textbook editors.

2. It begins after school is over and lessons have finished.

Connects play with the end of lessons.

3. The children wonder who will run fastest and who will laugh loudest.

Identifies both running and laughing.

4. It creates a joyful, playful mood. The children welcome the end of school and invite one another to run and laugh.

Names a plausible mood and supports it from the text.

5. The speaker and the person being addressed are included. The poem does not identify their individual names.

Identifies participants without inventing names.

6. The invitation to try suggests that both children can join the activity rather than only watch it.

Explains the inclusive invitation; does not claim a winner is named.

7. They mark the questions about who will run fastest and laugh loudest, making the challenges sound lively.

Connects punctuation with the two questions.

8. No. It raises the challenge and invites the children to try, but does not report a result.

Distinguishes an invitation from a reported outcome.

Teacher note: The book sample offers eight poem questions, with any five answered. These are a lesson practice pool, not a claim that this poem must be selected nationally.


### paragraph

My first day at my new school began with mixed feelings. I felt excited about meeting classmates, but I was also a little nervous. My mother took me to the school gate and wished me well. Inside, a teacher showed me the way to my classroom. A pupil smiled and offered me a seat beside her. During the break, she introduced me to two friends. They explained where we kept our books and how to borrow a library book. I asked questions whenever I was unsure. By the end of the day, the building no longer felt strange. I went home feeling welcome and hopeful. That day taught me how small acts of kindness can help a newcomer feel comfortable.

- Relevant first-person account with reasons for feelings

- Coherent sequence and paragraph unity

- Suitable vocabulary and comprehensible grammar

- Appropriate punctuation and the requested length

Teacher note: Original personal-writing example, not facts about Tarun. Students may use different experiences; do not require verbatim memorisation.
