## summary

A class safely observed water collecting near its school gate and kept records for five days. After noticing leaves over part of a drain, the pupils reported their notes to the headteacher. A maintenance team cleared the leaves while the children stayed away from the waste. The school continued checking because a single improvement was not enough to prove a lasting solution.

## story

Tanvir stayed on the dry path and called to the younger pupil to stop. The child heard him and came back towards the classroom. Tanvir then told a nearby teacher about the water. The teacher guided the children to a safe route and asked another adult to keep pupils away from the flooded area. Tanvir did not try to test the depth with his foot or search for a hidden drain. Later, the school arranged for the area to be checked by the people responsible for maintenance. The younger pupil thanked Tanvir for warning him. Tanvir understood that helping someone does not always mean doing a dangerous job yourself. Sometimes the best help is a clear warning and a quick report to a responsible adult.

## paragraph

Useful observation notes help us remember what we actually saw. Before beginning a school project, we should decide what information we need. We can write the date, time and place at the top of a page. A table is helpful when we want to compare the same details on several days. Our words should be clear, and we should separate observations from guesses. For example, seeing yellow leaves does not tell us their exact cause. If we miss a day, it is better to mark the information as missing than to invent a result. Working in a group also requires checking that everyone understands the headings. At the end, we can use the notes to explain our findings and the questions that remain. Good records do not answer everything, but they make our thinking easier to check and discuss.

Explains the purpose of observation notes
Gives relevant practical details
Distinguishes observation from an unsupported conclusion
Organises ideas and uses readable language

Joy: Shall we walk through it quickly?
Maliha: No. We cannot see what is underneath. Let us use the dry path.
Joy: Should we tell someone about it?
Maliha: Yes, we can report it to the teacher on duty.
Joy: I will explain where the water is. Could you come with me?
Maliha: Certainly. We should stay on this safe route.
Joy: Do we need to clear the drain ourselves?
Maliha: No. A responsible adult can arrange for trained people to check it.
Joy: All right. Let us report it now.
