# Lesson 3: At a Railway Station

Class 6 · English 1st Paper · Draft · Teacher review pending

Source: NCTB English for Today, revised for 2026. Printed 7–8 / PDF 12–13. Refer to the cited textbook passage/picture. No marks allocated; not a full model test.


## Teacher answers

Equivalent accurate wording is acceptable.


### mcq

1. A. To see Sajjad off

The narrator states the purpose was to see his friend off.

2. B. To Dhaka

Dhaka is the stated destination, not the unnamed departure station.

3. C. His elder sister

The passage identifies his elder sister’s house.

4. D. Holding her child’s hand tightly

The woman in the ticket queue held her child’s hand.

5. A. To sit down and rest

Their stated aim was rest.

6. B. A magazine

She was sitting with her luggage and reading a magazine.

7. C. Near a newspaper stall

The second paragraph names the newspaper stall.


### short-answers

1. There was a long line of people at the ticket counter.

2. They were talking loudly and drinking tea in front of the stall.

3. The waiting room was crowded too, so it might have been difficult to find a quiet place there.

4. He was trying to carry three heavy suitcases.

5. He picked up his suitcase, and he and the narrator said goodbye to each other.


### vocabulary

1. go with someone to their departure point and say goodbye

2. full of people; packed with people

3. a line of people waiting their turn

4. light

5. put down

Teacher note: The textbook matching exercise pairs quiet with busy; these contrast this station context but are not universal antonyms. Put down is a more direct opposite of pick up (lift) than drop. Accept drop where the task explicitly follows the book’s supplied choices.


### summary

The narrator visits a crowded railway station to see his friend Sajjad off on a journey to his elder sister’s house in Dhaka. People queue for tickets, drink tea, look for seats and manage their luggage. The friends watch the busy scene near a newspaper stall. When the train whistles and begins to leave, Sajjad picks up his suitcase and they say goodbye.

- Purpose of visit and Sajjad’s destination

- Representative details of the crowded station

- Departure and farewell

- Concise connected sentences without invented train times or station names


### picture

A train is beside a crowded platform. People are standing near the carriage doors. A person is carrying a load on their head. A child is standing beside an adult.

- Describes visible details

- Uses suitable present forms

- Does not invent destination, relationships or hidden actions

Teacher note: The textbook picture must be available for this task. More than one accurate description is acceptable.


### paragraph

Last Friday, I visited a book fair with my aunt. We walked slowly past the stalls and looked at colourful covers. Some visitors were comparing prices, while others were asking sellers about new books. A little boy was showing his mother a picture book. We stopped at a stall with stories for young readers. The seller helped me choose a short story collection. My aunt bought a book about birds. After paying, we put the books in our cloth bag and found a quiet place to sit. I read the first page of my new book before we went home. I enjoyed watching people share their interest in reading.

- Clear setting and sequence

- Relevant observations and personal actions

- Suitable past forms and connected sentences

- Original coherent account

Teacher note: Original imagined account based on the A4 writing skill, not an event reported about Sajjad.
