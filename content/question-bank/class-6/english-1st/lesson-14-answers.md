## Teacher answers

Accept equivalent accurate wording and supported interpretations.

### mcq

1. A. He saw his reflection in the river

The opening links his reflection with dissatisfaction about his mane.

2. B. He dipped his paw into the river

The narrative describes dipping his paw into water.

3. C. The giraffe

The giraffe suggests colour to match the moustache.

4. D. The vulture

Each animal offers a different appearance-related suggestion.

5. A. A paper crown

The hyena’s advice also concerns appearance.

6. B. Wisdom

The snake shifts attention from looks to wise leadership.

7. C. To be wise and stop worrying about his looks

The ending explicitly states this change of priorities.

### short-answers

1. He thought it looked too old and unruly for a king and wanted a new hairstyle.

2. The others suggested changes to his appearance. The snake said that wisdom mattered more than hairstyle for a king.

3. He smiled, kissed the snake and then decided not to worry about his looks.

4. It shows that he decides to value being a wise ruler rather than concentrating on his appearance.

5. Yes. A leader’s decisions and treatment of others matter more than a hairstyle. A different reasoned opinion is acceptable if it addresses the story.

### vocabulary

1. long hair on the neck of an animal such as a lion or horse

2. difficult to control or manage

3. make a loud deep sound

4. too concerned with one’s appearance or importance

5. making a prolonged s-like sound

### paragraph

At first, the lion worries that his mane does not look suitable for a king. The animals around him offer suggestions about colour, ribbons and a crown. These ideas keep his attention on appearance. The snake gives a different kind of advice by asking him to value wisdom. After considering it, the lion recognises that he has been vain. He shakes his mane back into place and promises to be wise instead of worrying about his looks. His change of mind suggests that good judgement matters more than decoration.
