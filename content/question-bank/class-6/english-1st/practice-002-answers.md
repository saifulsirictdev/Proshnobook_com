## story

Adil realised that he did not know which object the label belonged to. Two models stood close together, and either one might have been the right choice. He left the label on a clear table and told the teacher in charge. Together, they checked the list of exhibits prepared by the pupils. The list showed that the label belonged to a small model bridge, not the house beside it. The teacher helped Adil put it back in the correct place. Later, a visitor used the label to understand how the bridge had been built. Adil was glad that he had asked instead of guessing. He learned that even a small mistake can confuse other people, and that checking information is worth the time.

## paragraph

A responsible visitor enjoys a public place without making it difficult for others to enjoy it. Before a visit, we should learn the basic rules and ask an adult if anything is unclear. In a museum, we should not touch objects unless a sign or guide allows it. Speaking quietly helps other visitors listen and think. In a park, we can use the paths, protect plants and put our rubbish in a suitable bin. If there is no bin nearby, we can keep safe rubbish in a bag until we find one. We should also respect queues and leave space for people who need it. When we have a question, asking politely is better than making a careless guess or disturbing a display. Public places belong to a wider community, so our actions should show care for both people and shared resources.

## dialogue

Pupil: Excuse me, may I ask about this basket?
Guide: Of course. What would you like to know?
Pupil: The label describes its use, but I cannot find a date. When was it made?
Guide: I do not have a confirmed date at the moment.
Pupil: Should I write an approximate year in my notebook?
Guide: Please write that the date is not given. We should not guess.
Pupil: Is there another source we could check?
Guide: I can check the museum records and ask the curator.
Pupil: Thank you. I will keep the question in my notes.
Guide: You are welcome. Asking careful questions is part of learning.
