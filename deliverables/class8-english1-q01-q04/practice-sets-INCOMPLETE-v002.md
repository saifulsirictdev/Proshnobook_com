## Set 1

Read the text and answer questions 1 and 2.

Nakshi Kantha is a traditional embroidered quilt associated with Bangladesh and West Bengal. The name comes from the Bengali word *naksha*, meaning an artistic design. People use cloth and thread to create patterns on the quilt. Traditionally, quilts were made for family use, but today they are also made commercially and sold in shops. The craft became widely known after the publication of Jasimuddin’s poem *Nakshi Kanthar Math* in 1929.

**1. Choose the best answer from the alternatives.** 1×7=7

(a) Nakshi Kantha is a kind of — (i) embroidered quilt (ii) wooden toy (iii) metal pot (iv) musical instrument
(b) The word *naksha* means — (i) a river (ii) an artistic design (iii) a market (iv) a poem
(c) Which materials are mentioned for making the quilt? (i) clay and water (ii) paper and ink (iii) cloth and thread (iv) wood and nails
(d) Traditionally, the quilts were made for — (i) family use (ii) building roads (iii) growing crops (iv) catching fish
(e) The craft is associated with Bangladesh and — (i) Nepal (ii) West Bengal (iii) Sri Lanka (iv) Bhutan
(f) *Nakshi Kanthar Math* is a — (i) newspaper (ii) poem (iii) quilt shop (iv) village fair
(g) Today, the quilts are also — (i) made commercially (ii) grown in fields (iii) used as boats (iv) made only of metal

**2. Write short answers to the following questions.** 2×5=10

(a) What is Nakshi Kantha? (b) What does *naksha* mean? (c) What materials are mentioned in the passage? (d) How were the quilts traditionally used? (e) How did the craft become widely known?

Read the text and answer questions 3 and 4.

Rice is a staple food in Bangladesh. People often eat it with vegetables, lentils, fish or meat. Fish are found in rivers, lakes and ponds, and hilsa is popular in the country. Panta ilish is a traditional meal of rice soaked in water and served with fried hilsa. People may also eat dried fish, called shutki. Bangladesh has many traditional sweets and pithas, which are enjoyed on different occasions.

**3. Complete the passage with suitable words.** 1×5=5

Rice is a (a) ____ food in Bangladesh. It is often eaten with vegetables and (b) ____. Fish are found in rivers, lakes and (c) ____. Panta ilish is served with fried (d) ____. Dried fish is called (e) ____.

**4. Read the passage carefully and replace the following words with their suitable synonyms or antonyms.** 1×5=5

(a) staple (synonym); (b) popular (antonym); (c) traditional (synonym); (d) dried (antonym); (e) different (synonym).

**Answers:** 1. (a) i (b) ii (c) iii (d) i (e) ii (f) ii (g) i. 2. (a) A traditional embroidered quilt. (b) An artistic design. (c) Cloth and thread. (d) For family use. (e) After the publication of Jasimuddin’s poem *Nakshi Kanthar Math* in 1929. 3. (a) staple (b) lentils (c) ponds (d) hilsa (e) shutki. 4. (a) main (b) unpopular (c) customary (d) wet (e) various. Other contextually suitable answers may be accepted.

## Set 2

Read the text and answer questions 1 and 2.

Rina’s school has a small garden beside its playground. At the beginning of the term, students divided the garden into several beds and planted vegetables and flowers. Their teacher showed them how to loosen the soil and water the plants carefully. Each group visits the garden twice a week to remove weeds and observe the plants. The students record the changes they notice in notebooks. They hope to share the vegetables with families in their community when the crops are ready.

**1. Choose the best answer from the alternatives.** 1×7=7

(a) The garden is beside the — (i) railway station (ii) playground (iii) river (iv) hospital
(b) The students planted — (i) only trees (ii) vegetables and flowers (iii) only rice (iv) grass alone
(c) Who showed the students how to prepare the soil? (i) their teacher (ii) a shopkeeper (iii) a driver (iv) a doctor
(d) Each group visits the garden — (i) once a month (ii) daily (iii) twice a week (iv) once a year
(e) The students remove — (i) books (ii) weeds (iii) desks (iv) fences
(f) They record their observations in — (i) notebooks (ii) newspapers (iii) tickets (iv) envelopes
(g) The students hope to share vegetables with — (i) tourists only (ii) families in their community (iii) a distant factory (iv) nobody

**2. Write short answers to the following questions.** 2×5=10

(a) Where is the school garden? (b) What did the students plant? (c) What did the teacher show them? (d) Why do the groups visit the garden? (e) What do the students hope to do with the vegetables?

Read the text and answer questions 3 and 4.

A library gives readers access to many kinds of books. Some readers enjoy stories, while others prefer books about science or history. In a library, people can look for information in reference books and borrow other books for a limited time. A librarian helps readers find suitable materials. Everyone should return borrowed books on time and handle them carefully so that others can use them too.

**3. Complete the passage with suitable words.** 1×5=5

A library offers many kinds of (a) ____. Readers can find information in (b) ____ books. A (c) ____ helps them find suitable materials. Borrowed books should be returned on (d) ____ and handled (e) ____.

**4. Read the passage carefully and replace the following words with their suitable synonyms or antonyms.** 1×5=5

(a) enjoy (synonym); (b) limited (antonym); (c) suitable (synonym); (d) return (synonym); (e) carefully (antonym).

**Answers:** 1. (a) ii (b) ii (c) i (d) iii (e) ii (f) i (g) ii. 2. (a) Beside the playground. (b) Vegetables and flowers. (c) How to loosen the soil and water plants carefully. (d) To remove weeds and observe the plants. (e) Share them with community families when ready. 3. (a) books (b) reference (c) librarian (d) time (e) carefully. 4. (a) like (b) unlimited (c) appropriate (d) give back (e) carelessly.

## Set 3

Read the text and answer questions 1 and 2.

On Saturday morning, the volunteers collected litter in bags and separated recyclable items from other waste. An adult helped them arrange safe collection of the bags. The students also placed a notice asking visitors not to throw rubbish near the water. Their work showed that neighbours can cooperate to care for a shared place.

(d) They collected litter in — (i) bags (ii) books (iii) shoes (iv) cups
(e) What did they separate from other waste? (i) fish (ii) recyclable items (iii) flowers (iv) water
(f) Who helped arrange safe collection? (i) a visitor (ii) an adult (iii) a shop (iv) a bird
(g) The notice asked visitors not to — (i) read books (ii) visit neighbours (iii) throw rubbish near the water (iv) use the road

**2. Write short answers to the following questions.** 2×5=10

(a) What did the students want to do? (b) Where did they arrange the clean-up? (c) How did they collect the litter? (d) What did the notice ask visitors to do? (e) What did their work show?

Read the text and answer questions 3 and 4.

Clean water is important for health. People use water for drinking, cooking and washing. Water can become unsafe when waste or harmful germs enter it. Families should collect drinking water from a safe source and keep it in a clean, covered container. They should also use a clean utensil to take water from the container. These habits can help protect the water after collection.

**3. Complete the passage with suitable words.** 1×5=5

Clean water is important for (a) ____. Water is used for drinking, cooking and (b) ____. Waste and harmful (c) ____ can make it unsafe. Drinking water should be kept in a clean, (d) ____ container and taken out with a clean (e) ____.

**4. Read the passage carefully and replace the following words with their suitable synonyms or antonyms.** 1×5=5

(a) important (synonym); (b) unsafe (antonym); (c) harmful (antonym); (d) clean (antonym); (e) protect (synonym).

**Answers:** 1. (a) ii (b) i (c) iii (d) i (e) ii (f) ii (g) iii. 2. (a) Keep their neighbourhood clean. (b) Near a pond. (c) They collected it in bags. (d) Not to throw rubbish near the water. (e) Neighbours can cooperate to care for a shared place. 3. (a) health (b) washing (c) germs (d) covered (e) utensil. 4. (a) significant (b) safe (c) harmless (d) dirty (e) safeguard.
