paragraph|001|The Life of a Farmer|A farmer grows the food on which our everyday lives depend. In Bangladesh, farmers cultivate rice, vegetables, fruit and many other crops. Their work begins with preparing the soil and choosing suitable seeds. They must then water the fields, control pests responsibly and wait for the harvest. Rain, drought, floods and changes in market prices can make their income uncertain. Not all farmers own the land they cultivate, and some struggle to meet production costs. Better storage, fair access to markets, reliable advice and affordable finance can improve their position. Consumers can help by valuing agricultural work and reducing food waste. Farming also requires knowledge, planning and careful observation of nature. A farmer is therefore not merely a producer of crops but an important contributor to the nation's food security and economy. We should respect this work and support safer, more sustainable agriculture.
paragraph|002|Our National Flag|The national flag of Bangladesh is a green rectangle with a red disc. It represents our independent country and reminds us of the sacrifices made during the Liberation War. The green suggests the country's natural beauty and vitality, while the red is commonly associated with sacrifice and the rising sun. We see the flag at government buildings, educational institutions and national celebrations. On important occasions, our school raises it respectfully while students gather for the assembly. Learning about the flag should mean more than memorising its colours. It should encourage us to act honestly, respect other people and work for the common good. A flag represents all citizens, whatever their religion, occupation or background. We must handle it with care and follow the appropriate rules for its display. To me, respecting our national flag means trying to become a responsible and considerate citizen of Bangladesh.
paragraph|003|A School Magazine|A school magazine is a publication containing work by students and teachers. It may include stories, poems, essays, drawings, reports and photographs of school activities. Our magazine gives learners a chance to express ideas beyond their examination scripts. An editorial committee invites contributions and reads them carefully before making a selection. Teachers guide the process, but students should have meaningful responsibilities too. Writers learn to revise their language, check information and acknowledge the work of others. The magazine can also represent students whose interests are not always visible in the classroom. Producing it requires planning, cooperation and a realistic budget. Both printed and accessible digital copies can help more readers enjoy it. A good magazine avoids hurtful jokes and respects personal privacy. It becomes a record of school life as well as a space for creativity. I would like our magazine to include more local stories and student illustrations.
paragraph|004|Environmental Pollution|Environmental pollution occurs when harmful substances or excessive noise damage our surroundings. Smoke from vehicles and factories can reduce air quality, while untreated waste can pollute rivers and canals. Plastic litter blocks drains and harms animals. These problems affect health, livelihoods and the natural systems on which people depend. Their causes are often connected with careless consumption, weak waste management and poor enforcement of reasonable standards. Individual action matters, but households cannot solve every problem alone. Authorities and businesses must provide effective waste collection, cleaner production and safe public transport. Students can reduce unnecessary plastic, separate waste where facilities exist and take part in supervised clean-up activities. We should also avoid burning rubbish and wasting water or electricity. Protecting the environment is not a task for one special day. It requires practical habits, reliable information and cooperation throughout the year so that everyone can live in healthier surroundings.
paragraph|007|A Tea Stall|Last week, I spent an hour at a tea stall near our local market with a friend. The stall had a small counter, a kettle and several wooden benches. The owner prepared tea while another worker washed cups and served snacks. Customers included shopkeepers, students and people waiting for transport. Some discussed the weather and local news, while others simply enjoyed a short break. The place showed how a small business can become a meeting point for a neighbourhood. However, smoke from a nearby road and the lack of a covered waste bin made the surroundings less pleasant. Affordable food should also be prepared and served hygienically. We thanked the owner before leaving and put our wrappers in a bin. The visit helped me notice both the friendliness and the difficulties of an ordinary public place. A well-managed tea stall can provide refreshment, employment and a modest space for conversation.
paragraph|008|A Winter Morning|A winter morning in my village often begins quietly beneath a layer of mist. The distant trees look faint, and drops of dew shine on the grass. People wear warm clothes before leaving home for school, work or the market. Some enjoy homemade cakes and a cup of warm tea. As the sun rises, the mist gradually clears and the fields become bright. The season can be beautiful, but it is not equally comfortable for everyone. People without adequate clothing or shelter may suffer, especially during unusually cold weather. Farmers and outdoor workers must begin their duties despite the chill. We should not describe their hardship merely as part of a pretty scene. Sharing usable warm clothes through a responsible local group can be a practical kindness. I enjoy the calm and freshness of a winter morning while remembering that a pleasant season should also be safe and comfortable for others.
paragraph|010|Your School Library|Our school library is one of my favourite places on the campus. It contains textbooks, storybooks, reference works and a small collection of newspapers. The shelves are labelled so that students can find books without wasting time. A borrowing register helps the librarian keep track of loans. I usually visit during the lunch break to read or return a book. The room encourages quiet study, but it should also welcome questions from new readers. A useful library needs more than attractive shelves. It requires a varied collection, accessible seating and regular opening hours. Students should handle books carefully and return them on time so that others can use them. I would like the school to add more science books, local writing and materials suitable for different reading abilities. A good library makes learning less dependent on memorised notes and helps students develop curiosity, concentration and independent judgement.
paragraph|012|The Importance of Learning English|English is used in many forms of international communication, education and technology. Learning it can help us read different books, understand useful online materials and exchange ideas with people from other countries. For students, it is a practical skill rather than a measure of intelligence or personal worth. We can improve by reading suitable texts, listening carefully and using the language regularly. Making mistakes is a normal part of this process. Teachers can help by creating a supportive classroom where students practise speaking and writing instead of only memorising rules. Learning English should not require neglecting Bangla or other languages. A strong first language can support clear thinking and further language learning. Access to good teaching is also important, especially for learners with fewer resources. I want to learn English so that I can express myself confidently, explore knowledge and cooperate with others while remaining proud of my own language and culture.
paragraph|013|A Street Hawker|A street hawker sells goods while moving through streets or working from a small temporary space. Hawkers may offer vegetables, household items, toys or inexpensive clothes. Their voices and displays are familiar parts of many neighbourhoods. They make useful goods available close to people's homes and earn their living through considerable effort. However, uncertain income, bad weather and a lack of safe selling places can make the work difficult. Customers sometimes bargain harshly without considering the seller's costs. At the same time, pavements and roads must remain accessible to pedestrians, including people with disabilities. Fair local planning should consider both needs instead of treating hawkers with disrespect. Clean surroundings, honest prices and safe products are important for everyone. We can behave courteously and avoid making assumptions about a person's character from their occupation. Street hawkers deserve dignity, reasonable working conditions and opportunities to improve their livelihoods.
paragraph|014|The Life of a Rickshaw Puller|A rickshaw puller carries passengers and their belongings over short distances. In many parts of Bangladesh, this work helps people reach markets, schools and narrow streets where larger vehicles cannot easily go. Pulling a cycle rickshaw requires physical effort, patience and knowledge of local routes. Earnings may vary with the weather, demand and the cost of renting the vehicle. Some workers face unsafe roads and unfair treatment from passengers. It is wrong to assume that every rickshaw puller has the same life or circumstances. Each worker has individual responsibilities, skills and hopes. Passengers should agree on a fair fare, avoid overloading and speak respectfully. Better road planning, rest facilities and access to appropriate social support can improve working conditions. A useful description of this occupation should recognise contribution as well as hardship. The person helping us complete a journey deserves the same courtesy and dignity as any other worker.
paragraph|015|Your Visit to a Book Fair|This year, I visited a book fair in our town with my elder sister. Colourful stalls stood along a wide path, and visitors moved between them with books in their hands. We first looked at children's literature and then explored science and local history. At one stall, a bookseller helped me choose a collection of short stories within my budget. I also listened to a brief discussion about reading habits. The fair was enjoyable because it brought readers, writers and publishers into the same public space. However, clear signs, accessible paths and enough drinking water were necessary for everyone's comfort. I avoided buying books only because their covers looked attractive and read a few pages before deciding. We returned home with three books and many ideas for future reading. The visit reminded me that a book fair is not merely a market; it can encourage curiosity and a lasting relationship with books.
paragraph|018|Tree Plantation|Tree plantation means planting and caring for trees in suitable places. Trees provide shade, support wildlife and help protect soil. They also absorb carbon dioxide as they grow. Planting a sapling, however, is only the beginning of the task. Its survival depends on the species, the location, the season and continued care. A tree that is unsuitable for a narrow pavement or an electric line can create problems later. Our school can consult knowledgeable local people before planning a plantation programme. Students can help prepare the ground, water young trees and keep a simple record of their growth. Native species and a variety of trees can support local biodiversity. We should also protect existing mature trees rather than assuming that many new saplings immediately replace them. A successful programme is measured by healthy trees after several years, not by photographs taken on the planting day. Thoughtful care makes plantation genuinely useful.
paragraph|019|A Road Accident|One afternoon, I witnessed a collision near a busy crossing on my way home. A motorcycle and a small vehicle struck each other when traffic was moving in different directions. People gathered quickly, which made the road more crowded. An adult called for emergency assistance while another person warned approaching drivers. I stayed away from moving traffic and did not try to move an injured person without guidance. The incident was frightening, even though help arrived. It reminded me that speed, distraction and poor road design can combine to create danger. Drivers should obey traffic signals, and pedestrians need safe crossings rather than only warnings to be careful. Helmets and seat belts can reduce the risk of serious injury. Schools can teach students how to seek help safely. A road accident is not entertainment, so we should never crowd around victims or share their photographs without a valid reason and proper respect for privacy.
paragraph|020|Preventing and Responding to Dengue|Dengue is a viral illness transmitted mainly through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes. Preventing mosquito breeding is an important part of reducing its spread. We should empty or cover containers that collect water and help keep the surroundings free from discarded items that can hold rainwater. Suitable mosquito protection can reduce bites, including during daytime. A person with fever or other worrying symptoms should seek advice from a qualified health professional rather than rely on social-media remedies. Warning signs such as severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting or bleeding require urgent medical attention. Medicines should be taken only with appropriate advice; some commonly used painkillers are unsuitable when dengue is suspected. Schools and local authorities can organise practical awareness activities and regular inspections of shared spaces. Cleanliness alone cannot guarantee that nobody becomes ill, but coordinated prevention and timely professional care can reduce avoidable harm. Compassion matters too, especially when a family is worried about illness.

The fair gave local producers a chance to sell their work and brought neighbours together. It also reminded me that tradition depends on the people who continue these skills. A village fair is most enjoyable when it is welcoming rather than noisy or unsafe. I returned home appreciating both the creativity of local craftspeople and the effort required to organise a successful community event.
paragraph|026|Water Pollution|Water pollution occurs when harmful substances make water unsafe for people or other living things. Untreated sewage, industrial discharge, agricultural runoff and discarded waste can all contribute to the problem. A polluted river may lose fish, smell unpleasant and become unsuitable for many everyday uses. People who depend on it can suffer most even when they did not cause the damage. Prevention requires proper treatment of waste, regular monitoring and effective action against irresponsible disposal. Households should not pour harmful materials into drains or throw rubbish into canals. Safe drinking water needs appropriate testing and treatment; clear-looking water is not automatically safe. Students can raise awareness without making unsupported claims about a local water source. They can also join supervised activities to keep nearby water bodies free from litter. Protecting water is essential for health, agriculture and biodiversity. It should be treated as a shared responsibility rather than somebody else's problem.
paragraph|027|Deforestation|Deforestation is the large-scale removal of forests for other uses. It can occur when land is cleared for settlement, farming, infrastructure or commercial activities without adequate protection. Forest loss destroys habitats and can increase soil erosion. It also reduces the capacity of forests to store carbon and regulate local environmental conditions. People who depend on forest resources may lose important sources of food and income. The issue cannot be solved simply by blaming individuals who need land or fuel. Better planning must consider livelihoods as well as conservation. Existing forests should be protected through fair rules and community participation. Restoring damaged areas with suitable native species can help, but a new plantation does not immediately replace a mature forest. Consumers can reduce unnecessary use of paper and wood and choose responsibly sourced products where possible. By understanding these connections, students can support practical conservation rather than treating forests as an unlimited supply of land and materials.
paragraph|031|A Traffic Jam You Experienced|Last Thursday, our bus became trapped in a traffic jam on the way to town. For nearly an hour, vehicles moved only a few metres at a time. Passengers grew tired, and some worried about missing work or appointments. Through the window, I saw buses stopping away from designated places and vehicles trying to enter the same narrow lane. Roadworks had also reduced the available space. The experience showed that congestion has several causes rather than one simple explanation. Better public transport, sensible parking rules, well-managed construction and reliable traffic signals can all help. Pedestrians need safe routes too, so clearing a road should not mean forcing them into danger. Travellers can allow extra time, but authorities must improve the system instead of placing the entire burden on individuals. When our bus finally moved freely again, I felt relieved. The journey made the value of orderly, accessible transport very clear to me.
paragraph|036|A Moonlit Night|A moonlit night can make an ordinary place look surprisingly different. On a clear evening, I once sat in our courtyard and watched the moon rise above the trees. Its light fell softly on the path and reflected from a nearby pond. Familiar shapes became quiet outlines, and the air felt cooler after a warm day. I could hear insects and the occasional sound of someone returning home. The scene encouraged me to put away my phone and pay attention to my surroundings. Moonlight does not make every place safe, so I stayed close to home rather than wandering alone. I also remembered that people working at night may experience the same hours very differently. For me, that evening offered a peaceful break from study and noise. Its beauty lay not in anything expensive or unusual but in a few moments of careful observation of nature.
paragraph|048|Social Network Services|Social network services allow people to share information and communicate online. Privacy is another concern, especially when people post personal details or photographs without permission. I try to use strong passwords, limit unnecessary notifications and think before sharing anything publicly. If a message is threatening or upsetting, asking a trusted adult for help is better than responding impulsively. Schools can teach practical digital judgement instead of assuming that every online activity is harmful. Social networks should support our relationships and responsibilities, not replace them. Setting reasonable limits helps us benefit from these services while reducing their risks.
paragraph|049|Uses and Abuses of Mobile Phones|A mobile phone is a convenient tool for communication and access to information. Looking at a screen while crossing a road is dangerous, and late-night use may interfere with sleep. Fraudulent calls and misleading messages can also cause harm. Students should not share private information, passwords or verification codes with strangers. At home, we can agree on sensible times for study, rest and recreation without making the phone the centre of every activity. Its educational value depends on the quality of the material and the learner's purpose. A mobile phone is neither automatically harmful nor a solution to every difficulty. Responsible use means choosing helpful activities, protecting privacy and knowing when to put the device away.

This helps me feel more prepared in the morning. Early rising is useful when it is part of a balanced routine that includes rest, study, exercise and time with the people around us.

paragraph|069|Air Pollution|Air pollution is the presence of harmful substances in the air we breathe. Vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, construction dust and the burning of waste are common sources. Polluted air can irritate the eyes and throat and worsen respiratory or heart problems. Children, older people and outdoor workers may face particular difficulties, although the risks affect everyone. Solutions require cleaner transport, better control of industrial emissions and responsible management of construction sites. Planting trees can help improve surroundings, but it cannot replace reducing pollution at its source. Individuals should avoid burning rubbish and follow reliable local health advice when air quality is poor. Schools can adjust outdoor activities when necessary and keep learning spaces as well ventilated and clean as conditions allow. Authorities should publish understandable information so that people can make informed decisions. Clean air is a basic public need, and protecting it demands sustained action rather than occasional campaigns alone.
paragraph|073|Load Shedding|Load shedding means a planned interruption of electricity supply when demand and available supply cannot be balanced. Power failures may also occur for other reasons, so not every outage is load shedding. Interruptions can disturb study, household work, businesses and essential services. Students may find it difficult to read after dark, while small shops can lose customers or stored goods. Hospitals and other critical services need reliable backup arrangements. Families should use safe lighting and avoid placing candles near flammable materials. Better maintenance, efficient use of electricity and sound planning can reduce the problem. Investing in suitable energy sources and improving the network are also important. When interruptions are unavoidable, accurate schedules help people prepare. We should not waste electricity simply because it is available at a particular moment. Reliable and affordable power supports education, health and economic activity, making improvement a shared priority for consumers, suppliers and policymakers.
paragraph|075|The Qualities and Duties of an Ideal Student|An ideal student is not someone who never makes mistakes or always receives the highest marks. Beyond study, students can help keep their surroundings clean and take part in suitable games or creative activities. Rest and emotional well-being matter too; constant competition should not damage health. A responsible student avoids cheating and does not bully others. If a friend is struggling, kindness and practical support are more useful than ridicule. I want to develop these qualities gradually. The purpose of student life is to build knowledge and judgement while learning how to contribute fairly and considerately to the lives of others.
paragraph|079|Climate Change|Climate change refers to long-term changes in temperatures and weather patterns. Current global warming is mainly driven by human activities that release greenhouse gases, especially the burning of fossil fuels. These changes can increase risks from extreme heat, heavy rainfall and rising sea levels. Bangladesh faces serious challenges because many people live near rivers or the coast and depend on climate-sensitive livelihoods. However, the effects differ across places and communities. Reducing emissions and preparing for unavoidable changes are both necessary. Cleaner energy, better public transport and protection of forests can help limit future warming. Stronger buildings, early warnings and resilient agriculture can reduce harm. Students can learn from reliable scientific sources and avoid treating every unusual day of weather as proof of a particular climate trend. Climate action also requires fairness, since people who contribute least to the problem may face severe consequences. Cooperation is essential at local, national and international levels.
